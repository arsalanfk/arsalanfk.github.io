Trading and daily report

I trade a personal account at Interactive Brokers that has held up to about US$87K in capital. This page covers the daily market report I build to follow the market and my positions, and the account's results for the past twelve months, including the losses.

1. The daily report

A Python program runs every trading morning and produces a five-page briefing on the market and the stocks I follow. The first page can be read in a few minutes, and the detail follows on the later pages.

  • What moved and why: the day's main drivers, such as rates, economic data and company news, each with its source and the watchlist names it affects, alongside the S&P 500, VIX, modeled dealer gamma, oil and the 10-year yield.
  • Watchlist trend and levels: 15 stocks and funds, each classified by trend from settled closes (price against its 50-day and 200-day averages), with the nearest support and resistance and relative strength against QQQ over 60 sessions, followed by related names and comparables.
  • News, macro and calendar: company news with its read-through to the industry, macro and geopolitical topics, and a seven-session calendar of data releases and earnings, with dates checked against the publisher.
  • Fragility meter: eight indicators (breadth, dealer gamma, volatility term structure, skew, event density, concentration, credit spreads and cross-asset stress) scored red, amber or green. The report tracks the meter's own record, and because most red readings so far have been false alarms, it is read as a measure of vulnerability rather than a forecast.
  • Levels and movers: an SPY level ladder and options metrics modeled from open interest, and the morning's largest movers with their catalysts. Each mover is logged and its outcome is recorded in the following sessions.

Most positions were profitable. TSM was the best performer at +$4.2K, and selling call options on TQQQ and NVDX generated consistent premium income. However, two positions outweighed these gains. IBX and QBTX are 2x daily leveraged ETFs on single stocks, and together they lost $14.7K in realized and unrealized value, which is more than the +$8.7K earned by the other 23 tickers combined. The net result for the year was −$6.0K on a mark-to-market basis, after $312 in commissions.

The main lesson is that a daily-reset leveraged ETF on a single company carries two risks, the direction of the stock and the path it takes, and both work against the holder in a drawdown.

3. How it was built

The report is written in Python with python-docx, options analytics and charting libraries, and the code was developed with the help of AI tools. I wrote the report specification, decide what it tracks, and review every issue before making any decision on the account. All trading decisions are my own.
