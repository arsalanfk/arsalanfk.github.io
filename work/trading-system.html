How I trade

This page describes the written rules I trade by, the daily report that applies them, and the log I use to test whether the strategy works. The account is held at Interactive Brokers and has held up to about US$87K in capital. Results for the past twelve months, including the losses, are in section 4.

1. The rules

The rule set has nine sections and was finalized as version 1.0 in June 2026. Its priorities are, in order, protecting capital, compounding returns and learning from each trade. Position size is determined before any signal is considered.

Risk unitOne risk unit (1R) equals 0.5% of account equity and is updated weekly. Each tactical trade risks no more than 1R between entry and stop. The stop distance determines the position size, and the minimum stop is 1.25 times the five-day average true range.
Portfolio heatTotal open tactical risk is capped at 3R, with no more than two tactical positions at a time. A trade that would exceed either limit is not placed.
Factor capsLeveraged ETFs are counted at their full multiplier. Exposure to AI and semiconductors is capped at 60% of equity on a delta-adjusted basis, with leveraged products limited to 25% of that amount. Metals are capped at 20% and any single name at 15%.
Three lanesThe account is divided into three parts. The core holds unleveraged index funds and large-cap stocks, sized to withstand a 20% sector drawdown. Leveraged positions require a defined entry, a price stop, a time stop and a plan to scale out, and are never held open-ended. Hedges are bought only when implied volatility is in the bottom quarter of its 13-week range.
Playbook selectorEach morning, the estimated dealer-gamma position and the event calendar determine which of three playbooks applies: A (fading moves when gamma is positive), B (following trends when gamma is negative) or C (no new leverage within 24 hours of a major event). If the gamma estimate is outdated or contradicts price action, price action takes priority for that session.
TripwiresAn eight-indicator fragility measure controls leverage. Five of eight indicators in the red for two sessions cuts leveraged exposure in half, and six of eight, or a 2.5% daily index decline, reduces it to zero. Exposure is rebuilt only after three calm sessions, in halves, with leverage added last.
Kill switchA decline of 8% from the account's high within 20 sessions removes all leverage. It is followed by a five-session pause and a written review of every trade before trading resumes at half size.
Evidence gatesA new playbook trades on paper or at quarter size until 20 logged trades show positive expectancy after costs and a drawdown below 6R. It moves to half size until 40 trades and to full size after that. A playbook that fails a gate returns to the previous stage.
Override frictionAny departure from the rules must be recorded in the signal-change log before the order is placed. Unrecorded overrides are treated as emotional trading, and three in one month require a week without trading.

2. The daily report

A Python program runs every trading morning and produces a five-page report. The first page is a dashboard that can be read in a few minutes, and the supporting analysis follows on the later pages.

  • Data collection: closing and pre-market prices for a 20-stock watchlist, major indices, the VIX, oil, interest rates and gold; high-yield credit spreads from FRED; SPY and QQQ options data used to estimate dealer gamma; and a seven-day calendar of market events, with each date verified against its source.
  • Decision summary: a market-conditions panel (index level, VIX, net gamma, oil and the 10-year yield), a risk panel calculated from the rules (risk in use, factor exposure against limits, distance to the kill switch and the fragility reading), and a signal for each stock with the price level or event that would change it.
  • Screening: the day's largest movers are tested against a small-cap mean-reversion filter. Most are rejected, and every candidate is recorded with its two-day and five-day outcome, whether or not I traded it.
  • Quality control: the program enforces word limits and consistent terminology, then adds the day's movers, signal changes and fragility reading to the log.

3. Purpose of the log

The log tests whether the strategy has an edge before I increase position sizes. So far, the small-cap screen has recorded 74 candidates and very few have passed. I treat this as no evidence of an edge, so the screen remains for observation only until the results show otherwise. The fragility measure is tracked the same way. Each elevated reading is compared with the following five sessions, and because most readings have been false alarms, the measure is used to limit leverage rather than to forecast the market.

Most positions were profitable. TSM was the best performer at +$4.2K, and selling call options on TQQQ and NVDX generated consistent premium income. However, two positions outweighed these gains. IBX and QBTX are 2x daily leveraged ETFs on single stocks, and together they lost $14.7K in realized and unrealized value, which is more than the +$8.7K earned by the other 23 tickers combined. The net result for the year was −$6.0K on a mark-to-market basis, after $312 in commissions.

The main lesson is that a daily-reset leveraged ETF on a single company carries two risks, the direction of the stock and the path it takes, and both work against the holder in a drawdown. The rule set I wrote in June addresses this for tactical trades. Leveraged ETFs count at their full multiplier, leveraged positions require both a price stop and a time stop, and no single name may exceed 15% of equity.

One exception. QBTX is a long-term conviction position rather than a tactical trade, and it is held outside these limits. I have been adding to it on price weakness to lower my average cost, and as of the September 2026 statement it represents about half of the account. I plan to hold it through December based on my view that QBTS will recover. All other positions follow the rules, and the outcome of this decision will be reported in the next statement.

5. How it was built

The code, written in Python with python-docx, options analytics and charting libraries, was developed with the help of AI tools. I wrote the rules and the report specification, designed the entry, exit and sizing logic, and review every report before making any decision on the account. All trading decisions are my own.
