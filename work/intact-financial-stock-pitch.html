Intact Financial (TSX: IFC)

Our team presented Intact Financial to a panel of CFA charterholders on February 6, 2026. We were a team of five and recommended a Buy with a $311 target price, 21.9% above the share price of $255.62 at the time.

Investment thesis

  1. Scale and underwriting discipline. Intact is Canada's largest property and casualty insurer, with about 21% market share, more than double its nearest competitor. Over ten years it has outperformed the industry's return on equity by about 650 basis points, and it has kept combined ratios in the low 90s even in years with heavy catastrophe losses.
  2. A profitable distribution business. Intact's broker network and BrokerLink generated $524M in distribution income in 2024, growing about 17% per year since 2020, while requiring very little capital.
  3. Improving international results. The U.S. specialty business runs at an 83.6% combined ratio. In the UK and Ireland, the combined ratio improved from 99.3% in 2022 to 92.8% in 2024, and the business is moving from restructuring to growth.

Valuation

We valued Intact on price-to-book rather than a DCF or price-to-earnings. Insurer earnings are volatile because of catastrophe losses, reserve changes and investment results, while book value grows steadily through underwriting profit and retained capital. Applying a 2.5x multiple, close to the five-year average of 2.6x, to FY2027E book value per share of $124.37 supports the $311 target. Price-to-earnings comparables for eight Canadian, U.S. and UK insurers, sourced from S&P Capital IQ, were used as a cross-check and received no weight. Sell-side price targets received a 20% weight.

My contribution: ESG analysis

I was responsible for the ESG section of the report (slides 15 to 18). Rather than using a general checklist, I focused on the ESG factors that directly affect an insurer's financial results.

  • Environmental: for a property and casualty insurer, climate risk is the most important ESG factor. Catastrophe losses in 2024 were C$1.54B, a 7.1% catastrophe loss ratio, and the 1-in-100-year probable maximum loss is about 5.7% of book value. I linked these figures to underwriting results and reinsurance, and argued that the environmental score should be judged by how quickly the company reprices risk rather than by its office emissions.
  • Social: I assessed claims handling, affordability after severe loss years and workforce capacity, using about 31,000 employees, a 90.4% retention rate and roughly two complaints per 1,000 claims. These factors affect customer retention and reputation after a major catastrophe year.
  • Governance: ESG oversight is part of enterprise risk management at the board level, ESG metrics are included in executive compensation, and since January 2025 a formal AI governance framework has covered the more than 500 models used in pricing and claims.

Sustainalytics rates Intact at 19.3, which is low risk, with a moderate controversy score. I converted this into a 1–10 scorecard so the panel could compare the environmental, social and governance results directly.

Risks

The main risks are integration costs in the UK and Ireland following the DLG transaction, a softening pricing cycle combined with claims inflation from tariffs, and regulatory complexity across the UK, Ireland and Luxembourg. These are partly offset by the unification of brands and technology in the UK, limited exposure to large commercial accounts, and capital levels above board targets in every region.
