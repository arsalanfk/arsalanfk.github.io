Thesis

  1. Scale and underwriting discipline drive industry-leading returns. Intact is Canada's largest P&C insurer (~21% share, more than twice its nearest competitor), has beaten industry ROE by about 650 bps on a ten-year average, and runs combined ratios in the low 90s even in heavy-catastrophe years.
  2. Distribution is a second, capital-light earnings stream. Broker relationships and the BrokerLink platform generated $524M of distribution income in 2024, a ~17% CAGR since 2020.
  3. International operations extend the model. U.S. specialty lines run an 83.6% combined ratio; the UK&I book has been remediated from 99.3% (2022) to 92.8% (2024) and is turning from fix-it mode to growth.

Valuation

We valued Intact on price-to-book rather than DCF or P/E: for a P&C insurer, earnings are noisy (catastrophe losses, reserve development, investment swings), while book value compounds through underwriting profit and capital retention. A 2.5x multiple, in line with the five-year average of 2.6x, applied to FY2027E book value per share of $124.37 gives $311. P/E comparables across eight Canadian, U.S. and UK insurers (data from S&P Capital IQ) served as a cross-check and were given no weight in the final target; sell-side targets received 20%.

My section: ESG analysis

I owned the ESG chapter (slides 15–18). The approach was to treat ESG as a set of financially material pathways rather than a checklist:

  • Environmental: physical climate risk is the first-order factor for a P&C insurer. I tied 2024 catastrophe losses (C$1.54B, a 7.1% CAT loss ratio) and the 1-in-100 probable maximum loss (~5.7% of book value) to underwriting results and reinsurance structure, and argued the "E" is best judged through pricing agility, not operational footprint.
  • Social: claims-handling quality, affordability in high-loss years and workforce capacity (~31,000 employees, 90.4% retention, ~2 complaints per 1,000 claims) show up as retention and reputation during catastrophe years.
  • Governance: board-level ESG oversight inside enterprise risk management, ESG metrics in executive short-term incentives, and a formal AI-governance framework (effective January 2025) for the 500+ models used in pricing and claims.

Sustainalytics scores the company at 19.3 (low risk) with a moderate controversy rating; I converted those into a 1–10 scorecard so the panel could compare E, S and G at a glance.

Risks we flagged

UK&I integration drag from the DLG acquisition, softening in the P&C pricing cycle with tariff-driven claims inflation, and regulatory complexity across the UK, Ireland and Luxembourg. Mitigants: brand and technology unification in the UK, limited large-account exposure, and capital above board thresholds in every jurisdiction.
