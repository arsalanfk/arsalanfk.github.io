Vancouver Airbnb and the 90-night rule

This was a five-person team project in COMM 414. Using Inside Airbnb's September 2026 data for Vancouver, we took the position of a short-term rental regulator and built a Tableau dashboard to answer one question: has part of the market moved past the 90-night line, where short-term rental rules no longer apply?

Background

Since 2024, BC's Short-Term Rental Accommodations Act has limited nightly rentals to a host's principal residence and required provincial registration. In October 2024, the City of Vancouver aligned its bylaw with the Act and changed its definition of a short-term rental from a stay under 30 days to a stay under 90 consecutive days. A listing with a 90-night minimum stay is therefore outside both sets of rules. The audience for this analysis is City of Vancouver licensing and housing policy staff and the BC Ministry of Housing, which runs the provincial registry.

Where the long-stay listings are

Of the 6,096 listings, 1,805 (29.6%) require a stay of 90 nights or more, and 1,688 of them are set to exactly 90, the first number that clears the rule. Only 54 listings sit anywhere between 30 and 89 nights. Long-stay listings are concentrated in the West End, Fairview and Mount Pleasant, while the single-family west side has very few.

Long-stay share by neighbourhood

Long-stay listings make up 79% of listings in Fairview, 60% in the West End and 53% in Mount Pleasant. In the west-side house neighbourhoods the share is under 15%, with Kerrisdale lowest at 7%. The pattern follows the housing stock: the long-stay market grew where rental apartments are, not where houses are.

Registration and host size

63% of long-stay listings show no City or provincial registration number, compared with 5% of short-term listings, and 99% of the 1,064 listings with no licence information at all are long-stay. Because both the City and the province define a short-term rental as a stay under 90 days, a 90-night listing needs neither a licence nor a registration number, so the missing numbers mark the edge of the rule rather than non-compliance. The 54 listings between 30 and 89 nights, which do fall under the rule, are 78% registered.

Larger operators moved furthest. 52% of listings held by hosts with five or more listings are long-stay, compared with 23% for single-listing hosts. The three largest hosts, Artin Properties (150 listings), Shirin (73) and Level Vancouver (47), are all in that group.

Long-stay homes are priced like rentals

For entire homes, the median long-stay listing is $146 a night, compared with $348 for short-term listings, and the middle half of long-stay listings sits between $111 and $203. At the median, a long-stay unit costs about $4,400 for 30 nights, which is closer to a furnished rental than to a hotel. These units therefore compete with rental housing.

What this means for the audience

The 90-night segment is a housing-supply question rather than a licensing one. Hosts have adapted to the rule, so licence sweeps will find little. The most useful indicator to track is the share of listings set to exactly 90 nights, and the main policy levers are the threshold itself and the tenancy rules that apply above it. Enforcement attention is better spent on the small number of short-term listings with no registration number and those that claim an exemption.

Limitations: minimum nights is the host's setting, not how long guests actually stay. The licence field is what Airbnb displays, not a check against the City or provincial registry. The data is a single snapshot from September 17, 2026, so it shows the split but cannot prove that the 2024 rule change caused it. Listing locations are randomized by Inside Airbnb by up to about 150 metres.

Earlier work

This project builds on an individual Tableau assignment I completed in COMM 365 in February 2026, using an earlier extract of 4,001 Vancouver listings. That analysis found that 81% of listings were entire homes, that hosts with more than one listing operated 40% of all listings, and that a quarter of listings already required stays of 90 nights or more, which is the pattern this study examined in more depth.

Tools

Tableau: calculated fields for stay type, registration status and host size, Percent of Total table calculations, a symbol map, box-and-whisker plots with reference lines, parameters for the Top N hosts view, and a dashboard with a filter action. Python was used to verify every figure in the report against the Inside Airbnb file.
